# Generic Milestone Intelligence Checklist

This company-agnostic checklist is a reusable structure for gathering, validating, and maintaining the information needed to produce milestone decision briefing packets for federal contract opportunities.

It is intentionally stripped of company-specific template names, internal review-body names, CRM names, local file paths, and proprietary process labels. Use it for question structure, evidence expectations, and workflow design only.

## Usage Rules

- Track each question as `Answered`, `Partial`, `Gap`, or `Assumption`.
- For each answer, capture source citations, validation method, confidence, freshness, owner, and last-updated date.
- Recommendations can be made before all answers are complete when assumptions, confidence, gaps, and next actions are explicit.
- Separate missing capture work from missing Ariadne platform capability; the former should create action-plan items, while the latter can become future workflow, skill, view, adapter, or automation work.
- Treat opportunity-specific answers as Opportunity Knowledge until human-approved Insight Promotion makes them reusable.
- Update related capture artifacts and action plans after major intelligence breakthroughs, gate reviews, or material changes.
- Trigger stakeholder review when new information materially changes win probability, risk, competitive position, affordability, or gate readiness.

## 1. Opportunity Overview

- What is the opportunity name, source-system identifier, organization priority, intended role, and current acquisition stage?
- What are all key dates in the customer buying cycle, including notices, industry events, draft solicitation, final solicitation, questions deadline, proposal due date, oral presentations, award, start date, period of performance, option periods, and recompete horizon?
- What is the estimated contract value, ceiling, expected revenue, expected staffing level, contract type, and small-business or socioeconomic requirement profile?
- What is the scope of work, major deliverables, performance requirements, and strategic fit with the user's organization?
- Is this a new requirement, recompete, incumbent recompete, extension, or legacy pursuit, and what performance or contract-history context exists?
- What special considerations exist, such as security clearances, facilities, transition timing, warranties, intellectual property, organizational conflict of interest, or other approval constraints?

## 2. Customer Insight

- Who are the decision-makers, influencers, evaluators, contracting officials, technical leaders, gatekeepers, and coaches?
- What are the customer's stated and unstated needs, hot buttons, pain points, success metrics, mission drivers, buying history, and organizational constraints?
- What acquisition strategy has been signaled or can still be influenced?
- How does the customer appear to weigh price, technical merit, management approach, past performance, risk, and socioeconomic factors?
- What pricing, estimating, labor, level-of-effort, or qualification guidance has the customer provided or implied?
- How does the customer currently perceive the user's organization, incumbent, and likely competitors?
- What customer intelligence collection tasks remain open, who owns them, and when are they due?

## 3. Competitive Intelligence

- Who are the likely competitors, incumbent teams, non-incumbent teams, and teaming combinations?
- What is each competitor's market position, aggressiveness, impact of loss, preferred contract vehicles, pricing behavior, and likely messaging?
- What are each competitor's likely capabilities, subcontractors, key personnel, relevant experience, past performance, and customer perception?
- How does the user's organization compare against competitors across likely evaluation factors?
- How might competitors adapt their solution, pricing, staffing, teaming, or messaging in response to the user's moves?
- What is the current competitive rank order, what evidence supports it, and how has it changed since the last review?

## 4. Solution Strategy And Discriminators

- What is the current technical and management solution concept, and how does it map to the expected work structure and deliverables?
- What are the true discriminators: benefits the customer specifically values that the user's organization or team can credibly provide?
- What solution gaps exist, and how will they be closed through teaming, investment, hiring, process improvement, or solution changes?
- What are the design-to-cost targets, risk premiums, make-buy-workshare assumptions, and key personnel strategy?
- What past performance evidence can be used as proof for the proposed solution?
- How does the solution exploit strengths, mitigate weaknesses, address competitor strengths, and take advantage of competitor weaknesses?
- What visuals, charts, or graphics are needed to explain the solution in the milestone briefing packet?

## 5. Win Strategy And Action Plan

- What are the top win strategy themes, and how have they matured since the last milestone?
- What customer engagement, shaping, call planning, site visits, white papers, requests for information, or one-on-one activities have been completed?
- What measurable changes in customer perception, requirements, evaluation criteria, or positioning resulted from those actions?
- What is the action plan for every gap, including action, owner, due date, expected result, and related discriminator or benefit?
- What must be true to win at the current gate or milestone?
- What should the executive summary or bottom line upfront say about strategic fit, capture progress, funding, competitive position, and recommended action?

## 6. Partner Strategy

- Why is teaming needed, and what exact capability, past-performance, relationship, geographic, socioeconomic, or capacity gaps does it close?
- Which partners are under consideration or selected, and what unique value does each bring?
- What agreements, workshare assumptions, roles, responsibilities, and customer-facing integration plans are in place?
- What legal, organizational conflict of interest, intellectual property, risk allocation, or terms-and-conditions concerns remain?
- Are partners aligned with the win strategy and actively contributing to the solution, capabilities matrix, customer engagement, or proposal evidence?

## 7. Price-To-Win And Win Probability

- What is the current win probability, and what evidence supports it?
- What is the independent price-to-win view at minimum, target, and stretch capability levels?
- What customer-directed constraints shape the price, such as plug numbers, labor categories, level of effort, salary floors, staffing minimums, or required features?
- Which pricing variables most affect evaluated price, and what assumptions drive each variable?
- How does the current price position compare with price-to-win, customer expectations, competitor projections, and execution realism?
- What bid and proposal investment is required, and what is the current spend versus forecast?

## 8. Risks And Mitigation

- What risks exist across technical, schedule, transition, financial, staffing, legal, organizational conflict of interest, small business, protest, and execution margin areas?
- For each risk, what are the probability, impact, owner, mitigation plan, contingency, and residual risk?
- Which residual risks require leadership, operations, legal, contracts, pricing, or delivery acceptance?
- What opportunity-specific risks exist, such as facility clearance, compressed transition, incumbent advantage, scope ambiguity, key personnel scarcity, or unusual customer constraints?
- What external events could materially change the opportunity, customer, budget, competitive field, or delivery environment?

## 9. Status, Schedule, And Roadmap

- What is the current maturity status for each Core Capture Workstream?
- What changed since the previous milestone, and what feedback or decisions must be incorporated?
- What are the next steps, owners, due dates, dependencies, and expected outcomes?
- What staffing, recruiting, transition, solution, pricing, and artifact development plans are active?
- What red flags, exceptions, blocked decisions, or urgent leadership questions need attention?

## 10. Gate Readiness And Decision Criteria

- Have promotion or approval criteria for the current milestone been objectively met with evidence?
- Are strategic fit, customer access, conflict checks, capture plan maturity, teaming, win strategy, price-to-win, risk posture, funding, and proposal readiness sufficient for the requested decision?
- Is the developing proposal or artifact set compliant, compelling, and appropriately reviewed for the current stage?
- What is the formal recommendation: pursue, no-pursue, hold, bid, no-bid, price approval, or another decision?
- What remaining hurdles exist, and what decision package is ready for review?
